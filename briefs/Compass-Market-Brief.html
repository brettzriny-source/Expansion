How to read it

Purchase & operating cost — vertical

Net purchase price after the rebate we expect to capture (more rebate = cheaper) weighted 75%, plus operating economics (SPARK) at 25%. Where a utility runs a heat-pump-friendly tariff, SPARK is discounted by that rate’s modelled bill saving rather than left on the default rate. Indexed 0–100 across the 19; lower is cheaper to own.

Operational complexity — horizontal

The licensing regime. 1 means one statewide license; 5 means every county runs its own exam and application. A local business-license or tax layer adds administrative lift, not licensing lift, where noted.

Time to first permit

How long the licensing takes, and nothing else. The clock starts the day we file the first application and stops the day we can legally pull a permit — application review, exam scheduling, board sittings. Siting, hiring and selling all run alongside it, and qualifiers are hired before we file, so none of that is in the number. It is not time to first sale.

Rebate reality

The richest rebates cluster in Northeast programs — NY Clean Heat, NJ Building Decarb. Most Southeast and dual-load markets are thin-rebate, strong-SPARK plays, where the case rests on operating cost rather than incentives.

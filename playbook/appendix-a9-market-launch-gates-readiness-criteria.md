# Appendix A-9: Market Launch Gates & Readiness Criteria
The scorecard-based framework that governs when a market advances from one phase to the next — and prevents launches from proceeding before they're ready.
## Purpose
Markets don't launch on calendar dates. They launch when readiness gates are met. This appendix defines the six major gates (G0 through G5) plus the stabilization gate (G6) that structure every Jetson market launch. Each gate is a collection of pass/fail criteria — if any criterion is red, the market does not advance. No exceptions.
The Massachusetts expansion taught us what happens when gates are skipped or overridden: operational chaos, customer frustration, and months of recovery work. These gates exist because someone learned the hard way what breaks when you skip them.
> **Core principle:** Sequence first. Speed second. A market that opens two weeks late but hits every gate will outperform a market that rushes and skips steps by six months.
---
## How Gates Work
### Gate Structure
Each gate includes:
- **Gate Definition:** What milestone this gate represents
- **Owner:** Who is accountable for declaring the gate passed
- **Duration from Previous Gate:** Typical timeline (not a deadline)
- **Pass Criteria:** The specific, measurable requirements that must be met
- **Critical Path Dependencies:** Key activities from the Smartsheet that must complete
- **What Happens If Failed:** Escalation path and remediation process
### Gate Governance
**Who Declares a Gate Passed?**
- **G0–G4:** Market Launch Lead recommends; VP of Expansion approves
- **G5:** VP of Expansion and VP of Field Operations jointly approve
- **G6:** GM and VP of Expansion jointly sign handoff document
**What If a Gate Criterion Is Yellow or Red?**
- **Yellow (At Risk):** Gate can proceed with documented mitigation plan approved by VP of Expansion
- **Red (Blocked):** Gate does not pass. Timeline adjusts. No pressure to override.
**How Are Gates Tracked?**
Gates are tracked in two places:
1. **Smartsheet Master Timeline:** The source of truth for task completion and critical path
2. **Weekly Gate Scorecard:** A one-page red/yellow/green summary reviewed in the expansion weekly rhythm (Appendix A-2)

## G0: Market Selection & Budget Approval
**What It Means:** The market has been selected, assessed, and approved for launch. Budget and targets are locked. The countdown begins.
**Owner:** VP of Expansion (Brett Zriny)
**Typical Duration:** 10–12 business days
**Smartsheet Phase:** Phase 1 — Market Assessment
### Pass Criteria
| # | Criterion | Owner | Evidence Required |
|---|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | Warehouse location/zip code identified and validated | Expansion/Ops | Service area map showing 1-day install coverage |
| 2 | Rebate programs identified and eligibility confirmed | Finance | List of available rebates with $ amounts and application timelines |
| 3 | Loan/financing programs identified and partnerships scoped | Finance | Loan partner list with terms, rates, and setup requirements |
| 4 | License requirements mapped (business, contractor, permits) | Expansion/Ops | Licensing roadmap with application timelines and fees |
| 5 | Launch installation volume targets confirmed (soft launch + ramp) | Expansion/Ops | Volume targets by week for first 90 days |
| 6 | Capex/opex budget approved (real estate, fleet, inventory, headcount) | Expansion/Finance | Signed budget approval from CFO and CEO |
| 7 | Market launch charter signed | Expansion/Ops | Charter document with scope, timeline, success criteria |
### What Happens Next
- Market Launch Lead is assigned
- T-minus timeline is activated in Smartsheet
- Weekly expansion rhythm begins (Appendix A-2)
- Real estate search commences
### Common Failure Modes
| Failure | What Happens | The Fix |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Budget not approved | Launch stalls before it starts | Finance and Expansion align on ROI model and capex phasing before submission |
| Rebates underestimated | Customer pricing is wrong at launch | Finance validates rebate stack with utility and state agencies, not just web research |
| Volume targets misaligned with ops capacity | Either demand starves the market or ops can't fulfill | Ops validates install capacity (crew count × days × utilization) before targets are locked |

## G1: Real Estate Secured (Lease Signed)
**What It Means:** The warehouse facility is leased, insurance is in place, and the layout plan is approved. Physical deployment can begin.
**Owner:** Market Launch Lead (with VP of Expansion approval)
**Typical Duration from G0:** 35–50 days (varies significantly by market)
**Smartsheet Phase:** Phase 2 — Real Estate
### Pass Criteria
| # | Criterion | Owner | Evidence Required |
|---|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | Lease negotiated and fully executed | Expansion/Finance | Signed lease agreement with move-in date confirmed |
| 2 | Facility insurance and security deposit submitted | Finance | Certificate of insurance + proof of deposit payment |
| 3 | Warehouse occupancy date confirmed and within timeline | Expansion/Ops | Landlord confirmation of occupancy date ≤ T-30 from planned go-live |
| 4 | Facility layout, racking plan, and EV charger placement finalized | Expansion/Supply Chain | CAD drawing or floor plan with dimensions, racking layout, parking, charger locations |
| 5 | Site meets minimum requirements (ceiling height, dock door, power) | Expansion/Ops | Site inspection checklist completed (Appendix TBD) |
### Critical Path Dependencies (from Smartsheet)
- Warehouse tours completed
- Lease negotiation to execution (16 days — historically at-risk)
- Site racking, layout, parking, and EV charger plan defined
### What Happens Next
- Racking contractor engaged
- Utilities, internet, and trash service ordered
- Compliance and licensing applications submitted (can begin in parallel)
- Leadership hiring accelerates
### Common Failure Modes
| Failure | What Happens | The Fix |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Lease negotiation drags beyond timeline | Entire launch timeline shifts right | Expansion team sets a "walk-away date" early in negotiation; has backup site identified |
| Site requires expensive retrofits (power upgrade, structural) | Budget overrun or unsuitable facility | Site inspection checklist is completed BEFORE lease negotiation begins |
| Occupancy date slips after lease is signed | Racking, IT, and inventory timelines compress | Lease includes occupancy date penalty clause; backup plan for delayed possession |

## G2: Compliance & Leadership in Place
**What It Means:** The market has applied for all required licenses, and the core leadership team (GM, FSM, Warehouse Manager) is hired and onboarding.
**Owner:** Market Launch Lead
**Typical Duration from G1:** 10–15 days (overlaps with G3 preparation)
**Smartsheet Phase:** Phase 3 — Compliance and Business Set Up + Phase 4 — Talent Launch (Leadership)
### Pass Criteria
| # | Criterion | Owner | Evidence Required |
|---|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | Business license applications submitted | Expansion/Ops | Confirmation numbers and expected approval dates |
| 2 | Contractor/home improvement license applications submitted | Expansion/Ops | Confirmation numbers and expected approval dates |
| 3 | Rebate/preferred contractor applications submitted | Expansion/Ops | Application confirmation + timeline to approval |
| 4 | Loan partner setup initiated | Finance | Signed partnership agreements or applications in process |
| 5 | GM hired and offer accepted | People/TA | Signed offer letter + start date ≤ T-45 from go-live |
| 6 | FSM hired and offer accepted | People/TA | Signed offer letter + start date ≤ T-30 from go-live |
| 7 | Warehouse Manager hired and offer accepted | People/TA | Signed offer letter + start date ≤ T-30 from go-live |
### Critical Path Dependencies (from Smartsheet)
- Leadership jobs posted (GM, FSM, WH Manager)
- Leadership recruiting and interviews (26 days — at-risk item)
- Leadership hired milestone
### What Happens Next
- Leadership onboarding and shadow assignments in existing markets
- Ops and Warehouse Coordinator hiring begins
- Facility setup accelerates (racking, IT, office)
### Common Failure Modes
| Failure | What Happens | The Fix |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Licensing approval takes longer than expected | Cannot legally operate on planned go-live date | Expansion team submits applications as early as possible (T-90 or earlier); maintains contact with licensing authorities for status updates |
| GM hire falls through or delays | No local leader to build the team or take handoff | Expansion team maintains backup GM candidates through final interviews; considers interim GM from existing market if necessary |
| Leadership hired but not available until after critical build dates | Leadership misses facility setup, hiring, and training windows | Offer letters include required start dates; Expansion considers earlier start with temp housing if needed |

## G3: Facility Ready & Team Hired
**What It Means:** The warehouse is occupied, racking is installed, IT is live, and the full ops and warehouse coordinator team is hired and onboarding.
**Owner:** Market Launch Lead
**Typical Duration from G2:** 30–40 days
**Smartsheet Phase:** Phase 4 — Talent Launch (Ops Team) + Phase 5 — Facility Set-Up
### Pass Criteria
| # | Criterion | Owner | Evidence Required |
|---|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | Warehouse occupancy achieved | Expansion/Ops | Keys in hand, access confirmed |
| 2 | Racking installed and labeled (aisle/bay/level/master setup) | Expansion/Supply Chain | Photos of completed racking + NetSuite location master updated |
| 3 | IT installation complete (network, workstations, printing/scanning) | Expansion/IT | IT readiness checklist signed off (Appendix TBD) |
| 4 | Office setup complete (desks, meeting rooms, safety equipment) | Expansion/IT | Office photos + furniture/equipment checklist complete |
| 5 | EV chargers installed and operational | Expansion/Supply Chain | Charger testing complete, fleet charging plan validated |
| 6 | All ops and warehouse coordinator roles filled | People/TA | Signed offer letters for all positions + start dates confirmed |
| 7 | Background checks and onboarding paperwork complete | People/TA | All team members cleared to start |
| 8 | Utilities, internet, and trash service active | Expansion/IT | Service confirmation + account numbers documented |
### Critical Path Dependencies (from Smartsheet)
- Racking permit window (28 days — at-risk item)
- Racking installed (3 days — at-risk item)
- Racking setup and labeling (7 days)
- Ops + WH Coordinator hired milestone (at-risk item)
- IT installation complete
### What Happens Next
- Field ops team reports for onsite shadow/evaluation training
- Physical deployment accelerates (vehicles, inventory, kitting)
- Systems configuration begins (ServiceTitan, NetSuite)
### Common Failure Modes
| Failure | What Happens | The Fix |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Racking permit delayed by municipality | Warehouse cannot be used for inventory or kitting | Expansion team applies for permits immediately after lease signing; maintains contact with permitting office for expedited review if possible |
| Racking installation quality issues | Unsafe warehouse or unusable layout | Expansion team inspects racking upon installation against approved layout; holds contractor accountable for fixes before sign-off |
| Ops team hiring shortfall | Not enough installers to hit volume targets | TA and Expansion widen recruiting funnel early; consider temporary borrowing of installers from existing markets for launch support |

## G4: Physical Readiness & Training Complete
**What It Means:** Vehicles are outfitted, inventory is kitted and staged, all systems are configured, the team has completed JetStart training, and sales enablement is live. The market is operationally ready to install.
**Owner:** Market Launch Lead (with VP of Field Operations approval)
**Typical Duration from G3:** 30–40 days
**Smartsheet Phase:** Phase 6 — Physical Deployment + Phase 7 — Sales Enablement
### Pass Criteria
| # | Criterion | Owner | Evidence Required |
|---|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | Vehicles landed and readiness check complete | Expansion/Fleet | Vehicle delivery confirmation + standardized loadout checklist signed |
| 2 | Vehicle outfitting complete (racks, tools, branding, chargers tested) | Expansion/Fleet | Photos of outfitted vehicles + loadout verification |
| 3 | Initial inventory received and put away | Supply Chain | Receiving logs + cycle count complete in NetSuite |
| 4 | Installation kits built and staged | Warehouse | Kit BOM checklist complete + dry run completed |
| 5 | Mock install and truck load/unload time trials completed | Training/Ops | Mock install video or checklist + time trial results documented |
| 6 | Field ops team completed JetStart (Jetson University) training | Training/Ops | Training completion certificates for all installers |
| 7 | Field ops team passed certification (install quality, safety, customer interaction) | Training/Ops | Certification checklist signed by Training Lead |
| 8 | ServiceTitan dispatch workflows configured and tested | Warehouse/Ops | Test job created, scheduled, dispatched, and closed in ServiceTitan |
| 9 | NetSuite locations, items, and approvals configured | Warehouse/Ops | Test transactions completed (PO, receiving, kit build, cycle count) |
| 10 | Mobile devices set up (field MDM, logins, MFA, apps) | Warehouse/Ops | All field devices tested and handed to installers |
| 11 | Sales enablement complete (pricing, intake, phone tree, website, digital ads live) | Sales/Marketing/Expansion | Customer can request a quote and be scheduled in target service area |
| 12 | Training and launch readiness gate passed | Training/Ops | Final readiness review with VP of Expansion and VP of Field Operations |
### Critical Path Dependencies (from Smartsheet)
- Place Rivian van order (33 days — at-risk item, longest lead time)
- Vehicles landed (at-risk milestone)
- Order/receive/put-away inventory (22 days — at-risk item)
- Build installation kits + dry runs (12 days — at-risk item)
- Field ops to JetStart (12 days)
- Field ops ready to install (gate milestone)
- Sales start (gate milestone — cannot begin before G4 pass)
### What Happens Next
- Final pre-flight check (readiness staffing, permits, vehicles, training)
- Sales begins controlled volume (soft launch)
- First installations scheduled
### Common Failure Modes
| Failure | What Happens | The Fix |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Vehicle delivery delayed | No way to transport crew and equipment to jobsites | Fleet team orders vehicles at T-90 or earlier; has backup plan (rental vans with temporary branding) if delivery slips |
| Inventory shortfall or quality issues | Cannot build complete kits, or kits have defective parts | Supply Chain validates BOM and orders with sufficient lead time; conducts QC on all long-lead items upon receipt |
| Training certification failures | Team not ready to install safely and to quality standards | Training program includes buffer time for re-certification; Expansion considers bringing in certified installers from existing markets for launch support |
| Sales starts before ops is ready | Customers scheduled before facility, crew, or systems are ready (MA expansion lesson) | **NON-NEGOTIABLE:** Sales does not begin outbound until Market Launch Lead and VP of Field Operations jointly sign off on G4 pass. This is the single biggest lesson from Massachusetts. |

## G5: Go-Live — First Installation
**What It Means:** The market has completed its first successful installation. Operations are live.
**Owner:** VP of Expansion and VP of Field Operations (joint approval)
**Typical Duration from G4:** 1–3 days (first install scheduling)
**Smartsheet Phase:** Phase 8 — Operations Launch
### Pass Criteria
| # | Criterion | Owner | Evidence Required |
|---|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | First installation completed successfully | Ops | Install completion documentation in ServiceTitan + customer sign-off |
| 2 | Customer satisfaction confirmed (no major issues, willing to provide testimonial) | Ops | Customer Net Promoter Score (NPS) ≥ 8 OR follow-up call confirms satisfaction |
| 3 | Install met quality standards (QA checklist passed, no callbacks within 48 hours) | Ops | QA checklist complete + no immediate service requests |
| 4 | System commissioned and functional (Jetson Hub connected, app working) | Ops | Hub online in customer app + remote diagnostics functional |
| 5 | Installation time within target range (1-day install achieved) | Ops | Install start and end times logged + within 8-hour window |
### What Happens Next
- Daily launch war room begins (Section 8.4)
- Soft launch phase continues with controlled volume ramp
- Hypercare support period begins (daily standups, rapid issue resolution)
- Post-install quality checks on every job
### Common Failure Modes
| Failure | What Happens | The Fix |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| First install has major quality issue | Immediate callback, customer dissatisfaction, team confidence shaken | Launch war room escalates issue immediately; VP of Field Operations and Training Lead assess root cause; additional training or process adjustment before next install |
| First install takes significantly longer than expected | Labor cost blows budget, crew exhausted, scheduling confidence lost | Ops reviews install footage, identifies bottlenecks, conducts additional mock installs or brings in experienced installer support for next jobs |
| Customer has bad experience (communication, cleanliness, professionalism) | NPS tanks, bad review, reputation risk in new market | GM and Market Launch Lead conduct immediate customer recovery call; Ops retrains crew on customer interaction standards before next install |

## G6: Stabilization & Handoff to GM Playbook
**What It Means:** The market has proven it can operate consistently at quality and efficiency standards. The expansion team hands off to the GM, and the market transitions to GM Playbook governance.
**Owner:** GM and VP of Expansion (joint sign-off)
**Typical Duration from G5:** 60–90 days (data-driven, not calendar-driven)
**Smartsheet Phase:** Phase 9 — Post Launch Scaling
### Pass Criteria
These criteria are defined in detail in **Section 9.2: Stabilization Scorecard**. Summary:
| # | Criterion | Owner | Evidence Required |
|---|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | Install volume ramped to target (≥80% of planned weekly volume for 4 consecutive weeks) | Ops | ServiceTitan volume reports |
| 2 | Callback rate below threshold (≤5% within 30 days of install) | Ops | Callback tracking report |
| 3 | Quality score meets standard (QA checklist pass rate ≥95%) | Ops | QA audit results |
| 4 | Labor efficiency within target (install hours per job ≤ target +10%) | Ops | Time tracking data from ServiceTitan |
| 5 | Customer satisfaction meets standard (NPS ≥70, review average ≥4.5 stars) | Ops | NPS survey results + Google review average |
| 6 | Inventory accuracy ≥95% (cycle count results) | Warehouse | NetSuite cycle count variance reports |
| 7 | Team retention stable (≤10% turnover in first 90 days, excluding probation failures) | GM | HR turnover report |
| 8 | GM fully trained and confident in operations | GM | GM self-assessment + VP of Expansion validation |
| 9 | All systems stable (NetSuite, ServiceTitan, fleet, facility) | Ops/IT | No critical system issues in past 30 days |
| 10 | Lessons learned document completed | Expansion | Lessons learned submitted using Appendix A-8 template |
### What Happens Next
- Formal handoff checklist completed (Appendix A-6)
- Market Launch Lead and expansion team step out
- GM assumes full accountability under GM Playbook governance
- Expansion team conducts post-mortem and updates playbook
### Common Failure Modes
| Failure | What Happens | The Fix |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Premature handoff (stabilization criteria not met) | GM inherits operational debt; expansion team pulled into firefighting for months | **NON-NEGOTIABLE:** Handoff is scorecard-gated, not date-driven. If criteria aren't met, expansion team stays involved until they are. |
| Expansion team doesn't let go | GM can't fully own the market; accountability is blurred | Handoff has a formal end date. Once scorecard is met and checklist is signed, expansion steps out. Clean break. |
| Lessons learned not captured | Next launch repeats the same mistakes | Lessons learned document is a **required deliverable** for handoff. No handoff without it. |

## Gate Escalation & Override Process
### Standard Escalation Path
```
Gate Criterion Red or Yellow
    ↓
Market Launch Lead assesses severity and mitigation options
    ↓
If mitigation exists → Document in gate scorecard, proceed with VP of Expansion approval
    ↓
If no mitigation → Escalate to VP of Expansion
    ↓
VP of Expansion decides:
    - Accept risk and proceed with documented mitigation plan, OR
    - Adjust timeline to resolve issue before advancing
```
### Override Authority
**Who Can Override a Gate?**
- **G0–G4:** VP of Expansion (Brett Zriny) only
- **G5:** VP of Expansion AND VP of Field Operations (joint decision)
- **G6:** VP of Expansion AND GM (joint decision)
**When Should a Gate Be Overridden?**
Almost never. Gates exist because someone learned the hard way what breaks when you skip them. However, overrides are permitted when:
1. The specific risk is well-understood and manageable
2. A documented mitigation plan is in place
3. The business impact of delay exceeds the risk of proceeding
4. The override is explicitly approved in writing by the override authority
**Override Documentation:**
Every override must be documented in the weekly gate scorecard (Appendix A-2) with:
- Which criterion was overridden
- Why the override was necessary
- What mitigation plan is in place
- Who approved the override
- What the follow-up plan is to resolve the red item

## How to Use This Appendix
### For Market Launch Leads
- Use this as your **primary roadmap** for sequencing activities and knowing when to advance
- Review gate criteria at **T-14 days before each gate** to identify risks early
- Update the **weekly gate scorecard** (Appendix A-2) with red/yellow/green status for each criterion
- Escalate yellow or red items to VP of Expansion immediately — delays are not failures, but surprises are
### For Cross-Functional Partners
- Know which gate criteria you own (e.g., Finance owns G0 criteria 2, 3, 6 and G2 criterion 4)
- Treat your gate criteria as **non-negotiable deliverables**
- Communicate status proactively to the Market Launch Lead — don't wait to be asked
### For the VP of Expansion
- Use gates as **decision points** for greenlight, pause, or resource reallocation
- Review the weekly gate scorecard in the expansion weekly rhythm (Appendix A-2)
- Approve or deny gate advancement based on pass criteria, not pressure to hit calendar dates
### For GMs
- Understand that **G5 is not the finish line** — stabilization (G6) is
- Use G6 criteria as your **scorecard for readiness to take full ownership**
- Partner with the Market Launch Lead through G6 — this is a collaborative handoff, not a sudden cutover

## If You Only Remember 3 Things
1. **Gates are pass/fail, not aspirational.** If a criterion is red, the gate does not pass. Sequence first. Speed second.
2. **The handoff (G6) is scorecard-gated, not date-driven.** No amount of calendar pressure should force a premature handoff. The GM inherits a stable, running market — not operational debt.
3. **The Massachusetts expansion taught us: never start sales (G4) before ops is ready.** This is the most common and most costly gate violation. Sales does not begin until the Market Launch Lead and VP of Field Operations jointly approve G4 pass.

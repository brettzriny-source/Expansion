# 1.1 Centralized vs. Regional vs. Market-Level Ownership

> Who owns what when Jetson enters a new market — and how ownership shifts as the market matures.

## Purpose

Launching a new market requires coordination across nearly every function at Jetson — sales, customer ops, procurement, field operations, IT, marketing, and finance. Without crystal-clear ownership at every phase, work falls through cracks, timelines slip, and the market opens behind. This section defines the three layers of ownership in an expansion launch, explains what each layer is responsible for, and maps how ownership transfers as a market moves from planning through stabilization and into steady-state operations.

> **Core rule:** Expansion owns the launch. Operations owns the run. The handoff between them is not a date — it's a scorecard.

---

## 1) The Three Layers of Ownership

Every market launch operates with three distinct ownership layers. Understanding where each layer starts and stops is what prevents the two most common expansion failures: (1) nobody owns it, or (2) two teams think they own it.

### A) Centralized (HQ-Owned)

These functions are built and managed centrally out of HQ in Vancouver. They do not change per market — they scale horizontally as Jetson adds geography.

| Function | Owner | Expansion Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sales** | Angie Hirata | Ramps demand in coordination with the expansion timeline. Does not begin outbound marketing until the Market Launch Lead confirms operational readiness. |
| **Customer Success** | Ryan Delmaire's org | Prepares the pipeline: rebate enrollment, documentation, prerequisites. In new markets, works closely with the expansion team to understand state-specific rebate programs and eligibility rules. |
| **Project Coordination** | Ryan Delmaire's org | Owns permits, scheduling, and calendar fill. In new markets, must learn municipality-specific permitting requirements — the expansion team provides the initial mapping. |
| **Customer Support** | Ryan Delmaire's org | Post-install support, remote diagnosis, service dispatch, review escalation. Readiness for a new market must be confirmed before go-live. |
| **Procurement + Warehouse Strategy** | Mike Galbraith | BOM, kits, vendor management, NetSuite inventory. Partners with expansion on initial warehouse setup, racking, equipment orders, and supply chain readiness for the new location. |

> **Non-negotiable:** Central functions do not "turn on" for a new market until the Market Launch Lead confirms readiness gates are met. The MA expansion taught us what happens when sales starts before the warehouse is ready or before licensing is complete — customer frustration, quoting errors, and operational chaos. Sequence first. Speed second.

### B) Expansion Team (Launch-Owned)

The expansion team — reporting to Brett Zriny (VP of Expansion) — owns the market from selection through stabilization. This is the layer that exists specifically to get a market from zero to operational.

| Role | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| **VP of Expansion (Brett Zriny)** | Portfolio-level strategy: which markets, in what order, at what pace. Greenlight decisions. Resource allocation across simultaneous launches. Executive escalation when a launch is off-track. |
| **Market Launch Lead** | Single-threaded owner of one market launch. Owns the T-minus timeline, coordinates all cross-functional workstreams, runs the launch war room, and is accountable for hitting every readiness gate on time. Think of this as the "GM of the launch" — before the actual GM takes over. |
| **Regional Expansion Director** | [TBD — future role as Jetson scales to 5+ simultaneous launches] Multi-market oversight, pattern recognition across launches, coaching Market Launch Leads. |

The expansion team does **not** build permanent local teams or run steady-state operations. Their job is to stand up the market, prove it works, and hand it off.

### C) Local (Market-Level, GM-Owned)

Once hired, the new market GM begins building the local team and taking over day-to-day field execution. During the launch phase, the GM works **alongside** the Market Launch Lead — not independently. Ownership transfers formally during stabilization.

| Function | GM Owns (Once Hired) |
| --- | --- |
| **People & performance** | Local hiring (with expansion team support), probation, standards enforcement, daily field discipline |
| **Field execution** | Crew management via FSM, utilization, labor efficiency, time tracking truth |
| **Warehouse & fleet readiness** | Warehouse Manager reports to GM; trucks organized, charged, stocked |
| **Quality** | Execution quality in the home, callbacks, audits, coaching |
| **Local reputation** | Google reviews, fast recovery on low-star reviews, community presence |

> **Note:** In some launches, the GM may be hired early enough to participate in facility setup, fleet procurement, and initial hiring. In others, the expansion team handles these and the GM inherits a largely built market. The exact split depends on timing and is defined in the market's T-minus plan (see Section 4.1).

## 2) How Ownership Shifts Across Launch Phases

Ownership is not static. It moves as the market matures through four phases:

| Phase | Primary Owner | Supporting | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Phase 1: Selection & Planning** | VP of Expansion | Cross-functional leads (Procurement, IT, Marketing, Finance) | 8–12 weeks before soft launch [TBD — confirm with Brett] |
| **Phase 2: Pre-Launch Build** | Market Launch Lead | Procurement (warehouse), HR (hiring), IT (systems), local GM (if hired) | 6–8 weeks before soft launch [TBD] |
| **Phase 3: Soft Launch & Go-Live** | Market Launch Lead + GM (shared) | All central functions active, heightened cadence | First 4–8 weeks of operations [TBD] |
| **Phase 4: Stabilization & Handoff** | GM (primary) with Market Launch Lead (oversight) | Central functions in steady-state mode | Until stabilization scorecard is met (see Section 9.2) |

### A) Phase 1 — Selection & Planning

The expansion team owns everything. The market doesn't have people, a facility, or systems yet. The VP of Expansion makes the greenlight decision based on the market scoring model (Section 3.1) and greenlight criteria (Section 3.2). Once approved, a Market Launch Lead is assigned and the T-minus countdown begins.

### B) Phase 2 — Pre-Launch Build

The Market Launch Lead is the single-threaded owner. They coordinate:

- Facility lease, warehouse setup, and racking (with Procurement/Mike Galbraith)
- Licensing, permits, entity formation, and insurance
- Fleet procurement and charger installation
- Systems provisioning (Zuper, HubSpot, Rippling, NetSuite)
- GM and key hire recruiting (with HR)
- Rebate and incentive program enrollment
- Service area mapping and marketing readiness

This is the phase where the MA lessons are most critical. Every item has a readiness gate. Nothing moves to the next phase until the gate is passed.

### C) Phase 3 — Soft Launch & Go-Live

Ownership is **shared** between the Market Launch Lead and the GM. The Market Launch Lead runs the war room (Section 8.4) and tracks launch metrics. The GM runs daily field operations. Central functions (Sales, Customer Ops, Support) are active but operating at controlled volume during soft launch.

This is not a rehearsal — it's live operations at controlled volume. The goal is to prove the system works before opening the throttle.

### D) Phase 4 — Stabilization & Handoff

The GM takes primary ownership. The Market Launch Lead shifts to oversight mode — monitoring the stabilization scorecard, flagging risks, and preparing the formal handoff documentation. Once the stabilization criteria are met (Section 9.2), the market officially transitions to **GM Playbook governance** and the expansion team steps out.

## 3) What Expansion Does NOT Own (Explicit Boundaries)

Clarity on what the expansion team does **not** own is just as important as what it does. These boundaries prevent scope creep and keep accountability clean.

| Expansion Does NOT Own | Who Does |
| --- | --- |
| Steady-state field operations after handoff | GM (governed by GM Playbook) |
| Sales strategy, pricing, or offer design | Sales (Angie Hirata) |
| Customer Ops processes (CS, PC, Support) | Customer Ops (Ryan Delmaire) |
| Procurement decisions, vendor selection, BOM changes | Procurement (Mike Galbraith) |
| Product design, Jetson Hub, or software | Product / Engineering |
| Company-wide HR policies, compensation bands | People Ops / HR |
| Safety program design | Cameron (VP Field Ops) |

The expansion team **coordinates** with all of these functions during a launch. It **embeds** representatives from each function into the launch war room. But it does not override their decision rights or take over their work.

> **Principle:** The expansion team is a launch accelerator, not a shadow operations team. If you find yourself running a function instead of coordinating it, you've crossed the line.

## 4) The Handoff: Expansion → Steady-State

The handoff is the most important moment in a market's lifecycle. Get it right and the GM inherits a clean, running operation. Get it wrong and the market spends months recovering from launch debt.

### A) The handoff is not a date — it's a scorecard.

A market transitions to GM Playbook governance when it meets the stabilization criteria defined in Section 9.2. Until those criteria are met, the expansion team retains oversight — even if the GM is running day-to-day operations.

### B) What gets handed off:

- All facility, fleet, and equipment documentation
- Licensing and permit records (with renewal dates and contacts)
- Hiring pipeline status and team roster
- Systems access and configuration documentation
- Rebate program enrollment status and contacts
- Service area map and municipality-specific permitting notes
- Lessons learned from the launch
- Open issues and risk register

### C) What the GM inherits:

- Full accountability for the market under GM Playbook governance
- A relationship with central functions (Sales, Customer Ops, Procurement, Support) that was established during launch
- A team that was hired, trained, and certified during the expansion phase
- A facility, warehouse, and fleet that meet Jetson standards

## 5) Common Failure Modes (And the Fix)

| Failure Mode | What Happens | The Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **"Nobody owns it"** | A critical task (e.g., charger installation, rebate enrollment) sits between two teams and neither drives it to completion. | Every task in the T-minus plan has a single owner with a due date. The Market Launch Lead reviews ownership gaps weekly. |
| **"Two teams own it"** | Expansion and a central function both think they're leading the same workstream, creating confusion and duplicated effort. | RACI matrix (Section 1.2) defines A/R/C/I for every launch activity. One A, always. |
| **"Sales starts before ops is ready"** | Customers are sold and scheduled before the warehouse, licensing, or crew are confirmed. Leads to quoting errors, scheduling chaos, and bad first impressions. | Readiness gates are enforced. Sales does not begin outbound until the Market Launch Lead signs off on operational readiness. This is the single biggest lesson from MA. |
| **"The GM inherits a mess"** | Expansion hands off too early or without documentation. The GM spends months cleaning up launch debt instead of running the market. | Handoff is scorecard-gated, not date-driven. The stabilization criteria (Section 9.2) must be met. Handoff checklist (Appendix A-6) is completed and signed by both parties. |
| **"Expansion never lets go"** | The expansion team stays involved past stabilization, creating confusion about who is accountable. The GM can't fully own the market. | The handoff has a formal end date. Once the stabilization scorecard is met and the handoff checklist is signed, expansion steps out. Clean break. |

---

## If You Only Remember 3 Things

1. **Three layers of ownership: HQ centralizes the machine, Expansion owns the launch, the GM owns the run.** Know which layer you're operating in and what it owns.
2. **Ownership shifts across four phases** — from Expansion-led planning through shared launch execution to GM-led steady-state. The transition is gated by a stabilization scorecard, not a calendar date.
3. **The MA expansion taught us: sequence first, speed second.** Don't turn on sales before the warehouse, licensing, and first crew are confirmed. Every readiness gate exists because someone learned the hard way what happens when you skip it.

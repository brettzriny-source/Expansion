# Appendix A-1: Market Launch Master Checklist

> The single T-minus countdown that governs every Jetson market launch — from greenlight to first install.

## Purpose

This is the ONE checklist. Every new market launch follows this T-minus countdown. It sequences every critical workstream — legal, facilities, fleet, people, systems, licensing, and go-to-market — into a single timeline with clear owners, gates, and deadlines. If it's not on this checklist, it doesn't block launch. If it is on this checklist and it's not done, you don't launch.

This checklist is the Market Launch Lead's primary operating document. It gets reviewed in every war room, every weekly sync, and every readiness gate review. Print it. Pin it to your wall. Live in it.

> **Core rule:** Nothing launches without every gate on this checklist cleared. No exceptions, no "we'll fix it after go-live," no heroics. The MA expansion taught us what happens when you skip gates — you spend months cleaning up launch debt instead of growing the market.

---

## Phase Summary

| Phase | Timeline | Primary Owner | Key Gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Phase 1: Market Selection & Approval** | T-90 to T-60 | VP of Expansion | Greenlight decision + Market Launch Lead assigned |
| **Phase 2: Pre-Launch Build** | T-60 to T-30 | Market Launch Lead | Facility, fleet, licensing, hiring, and systems all in motion with confirmed timelines |
| **Phase 3: Readiness Confirmation** | T-30 to T-14 | Market Launch Lead + GM (if hired) | All critical infrastructure operational; first crew in training |
| **Phase 4: Final Readiness** | T-14 to T-0 | Market Launch Lead + GM | Go/No-Go decision by VP of Expansion |
| **Phase 5: Post-Launch** | Week 1–4 | Market Launch Lead + GM (shared) | Controlled volume, daily war room, quality audits on 100% of jobs |

> **Non-negotiable:** T-0 is soft launch date — not "everything is perfect" date. But the readiness gates below are non-negotiable before any customer-facing work begins. Soft launch means controlled volume, not unprepared volume.

## Phase 1: Market Selection & Approval (T-90 to T-60)

This phase is VP of Expansion-led. The market goes from candidate to approved. No resources are committed until greenlight.

### A) Market Scoring & Analysis (~2 weeks)

- [ ] Market scoring model completed per Section 3.1 (TAM, regulatory environment, labor availability, competitive landscape, utility rebate programs, weather/seasonality)
- [ ] Financial model built — projected revenue ramp, cost-to-launch, and breakeven timeline [TBD — confirm with Brett on standard financial model template]
- [ ] Comparable market analysis complete (which existing Jetson markets does this most resemble?)

### B) Greenlight Decision (~1 week)

- [ ] Market scoring package presented to VP of Expansion
- [ ] Greenlight / No-Go decision made by VP of Expansion
- [ ] If greenlight: market added to the active launch portfolio

### C) Launch Setup (~2 weeks)

- [ ] Market Launch Lead assigned and briefed
- [ ] T-minus countdown calendar created with all milestones loaded
- [ ] Budget approved by Finance — initial launch budget and monthly burn authorized
- [ ] Legal entity formation initiated (state-level LLC or branch registration) [TBD — confirm with Brett on legal entity strategy per state]
- [ ] Kickoff meeting held with all cross-functional leads (Procurement, HR, IT, Sales, Customer Ops, Marketing, Finance)

> **Note:** Phase 1 timelines are approximate. Some markets with complex regulatory environments may need T-120 or longer for the selection phase. The Market Launch Lead should flag this in the kickoff meeting and adjust the master timeline accordingly. [TBD — confirm with Brett on extended timeline thresholds]

## Phase 2: Pre-Launch Build (T-60 to T-30)

This is where the market gets built. The Market Launch Lead is the single-threaded owner — coordinating every workstream, tracking every dependency, and escalating blockers immediately. This phase has the most parallel workstreams and the most cross-functional dependencies.

### A) Facility & Warehouse (~4–6 weeks lead time)

- [ ] Market area scouted for warehouse locations (proximity to service area, zoning for commercial/light industrial, EV charger feasibility)
- [ ] Facility lease signed
- [ ] Warehouse racking and shelving ordered (coordinate with Mike Galbraith / Procurement)
- [ ] Security system installed (cameras, access control, alarm)
- [ ] Network/internet installed and tested
- [ ] EV charger installation scheduled at warehouse [TBD — confirm with Brett on charger vendor and lead times per market]

### B) Fleet & Equipment (~6–8 weeks lead time)

- [ ] Fleet vehicles ordered (type, quantity per the market launch plan)
- [ ] Vehicle upfitting scheduled (racking, branding, tool loadout)
- [ ] EV charger installation scheduled at warehouse (if fleet is electric)
- [ ] Tool and equipment kits ordered per standard BOM

### C) Licensing, Permits & Legal (~4–8 weeks, highly variable)

- [ ] State contractor licensing application submitted
- [ ] Municipal business licenses identified and applications submitted
- [ ] Electrical permits — research municipality-specific requirements and document
- [ ] Insurance policies confirmed for new market (general liability, workers' comp, auto, bonding)
- [ ] Bonding confirmed where required by state or municipality

> **Warning:** Licensing is the #1 timeline risk in most markets. Some states take 8–12 weeks to process contractor licenses. Some municipalities have additional requirements. Start this on Day 1 of Phase 2. Do not assume it will be fast. The Market Launch Lead should have a licensing tracker with weekly status updates.

### D) People & Recruiting (~4–8 weeks)

- [ ] GM job posted and recruiting launched (coordinate with HR)
- [ ] Field team recruiting pipeline opened (Crew Leads, Electricians, Apprentices)
- [ ] Warehouse Manager job posted [TBD — confirm with Brett if WH Manager is hired pre-launch or if GM handles initially]
- [ ] Compensation and offer packages confirmed per market (cost of living adjustments, competitive analysis)
- [ ] Trade school and workforce program partnerships explored

### E) Systems & Technology (~2–3 weeks)

- [ ] HubSpot: new market/territory configured
- [ ] Zuper: service area, job types, workflows configured
- [ ] Rippling: new entity/location set up for payroll and HR
- [ ] NetSuite: warehouse location, inventory items, and procurement workflows configured
- [ ] IT provisioning request submitted for all new hire equipment (laptops, tablets, phones)

### F) Go-to-Market Preparation (~3–4 weeks)

- [ ] Service area mapping complete — zip codes, drive-time boundaries, exclusion zones documented
- [ ] Rebate and incentive program enrollment started (utility programs, state programs, federal programs) — coordinate with Customer Success
- [ ] Marketing materials localized (service area, local phone number, local address)
- [ ] Google Business Profile created and verified
- [ ] Local SEO and digital presence initiated

## Phase 3: Readiness Confirmation (T-30 to T-14)

Everything ordered in Phase 2 must now be received, installed, tested, and confirmed. This is the phase where the Market Launch Lead transitions from "building" to "confirming." If something is not ready, this is where you escalate — not at T-7.

### A) Facility & Warehouse Readiness

- [ ] Warehouse fully operational — racking installed, security active, network live, branding up
- [ ] Warehouse stocked with initial inventory (coordinate with Procurement on initial BOM/kit shipment)
- [ ] EV chargers installed and operational at warehouse
- [ ] Warehouse address confirmed in all systems (Zuper, NetSuite, HubSpot)

### B) Fleet Readiness

- [ ] Fleet vehicles received, registered, and insured
- [ ] Vehicles upfitted — racking, tools, branding complete
- [ ] Chargers operational and tested (if EV fleet)
- [ ] Fleet tracking / telematics active [TBD — confirm with Brett on telematics vendor]

### C) People Readiness

- [ ] GM hired and actively onboarding (30/60/90 plan initiated per Appendix A-5)
- [ ] At minimum 1 full crew hired (Crew Lead + 1–2 Electricians/Apprentices) and in training
- [ ] Training plan confirmed — crew scheduled for Denver training center or equivalent [TBD — confirm with Brett on training location strategy for remote markets]
- [ ] All new hires provisioned in Rippling, Zuper, and applicable systems

### D) Systems & Technology Readiness

- [ ] All systems provisioned and tested with real data (test jobs created in Zuper, test contacts in HubSpot)
- [ ] Scheduling workflows confirmed — a job can be created, assigned, scheduled, and completed end-to-end
- [ ] NetSuite inventory reflects actual warehouse stock
- [ ] IT equipment deployed to all new hires

### E) Licensing & Compliance

- [ ] State contractor license confirmed (in hand or confirmed approval with license number)
- [ ] Municipal business licenses confirmed
- [ ] Insurance certificates updated to include new market
- [ ] Bonding confirmed (if required)

### F) Go-to-Market Readiness

- [ ] Rebate program enrollment confirmed — Jetson is an approved contractor/installer in applicable programs
- [ ] Marketing materials printed/shipped and digital assets live
- [ ] Customer Support team briefed on new market — service area, products offered, escalation contacts, launch timeline
- [ ] Sales team briefed and ready to begin controlled outbound at soft launch

> **Note:** If any Phase 3 item is not confirmed by T-14, the Market Launch Lead must escalate to the VP of Expansion immediately. A delay at T-14 means a potential launch delay — and that decision belongs to the VP of Expansion, not the Market Launch Lead. ## Phase 4: Final Readiness (T-14 to T-0)

The final two weeks. Everything should be confirmed and operational. This phase is about validation, testing, and the formal Go/No-Go decision. No new workstreams should be starting here — if something is still "in progress" at T-14, it's a red flag.

### A) Readiness Gate Review (T-14)

- [ ] Formal readiness gate review held with all functional leads (Expansion, Procurement, HR, IT, Sales, Customer Ops, Finance, Marketing)
- [ ] Every Phase 1–3 checklist item reviewed and confirmed green/yellow/red
- [ ] Any yellow or red items have documented mitigation plans with owners and deadlines
- [ ] Readiness gate review summary shared with VP of Expansion

### B) Crew Certification & Test Installs (T-14 to T-7)

- [ ] First crew certified — completed training (Denver or equivalent), passed all assessments
- [ ] Test installs completed (internal installs or beta customers) — minimum 2–3 test jobs [TBD — confirm with Brett on minimum test install count]
- [ ] Test install quality audited — meets Jetson standards
- [ ] Any issues from test installs documented and resolved

### C) Operational Dry Run (T-7 to T-3)

- [ ] Warehouse stocked with initial BOM/kits for first 2–4 weeks of projected volume
- [ ] Full end-to-end operational dry run completed — from customer booking through job completion, invoicing, and rebate submission
- [ ] War room cadence established — daily standup time, attendees, agenda, and tools confirmed (see Appendix A-7)
- [ ] Escalation protocols tested — everyone knows how to flag issues and who to call

### D) Go/No-Go Decision (T-3 to T-0)

- [ ] Go/No-Go decision meeting held with VP of Expansion and all functional leads
- [ ] VP of Expansion makes formal Go/No-Go call
- [ ] If Go: soft launch date confirmed and communicated to all teams
- [ ] If No-Go: delay documented with revised timeline, root cause, and remediation plan
- [ ] Sales team given final green light to begin controlled outbound

> **Core rule:** The Go/No-Go decision belongs to the VP of Expansion. No one else can authorize a market to go live. If there is any doubt, the answer is No-Go until doubt is resolved. Launching a market that isn't ready costs more than a two-week delay — it costs months of recovery and reputation damage.

## Phase 5: Post-Launch (Week 1–4)

The market is live — at controlled volume. This is not "business as usual." This is launch mode with heightened oversight, daily cadence, and quality controls on every single job.

### A) Soft Launch Operations (Week 1–2)

- [ ] Soft launch begins — controlled volume (target: [TBD — confirm with Brett on standard soft launch volume targets, e.g., 3–5 jobs/week])
- [ ] Daily war room active — Market Launch Lead runs daily standup with GM, Sales, Customer Ops, and Procurement
- [ ] Every job gets a quality audit — no exceptions during soft launch
- [ ] Customer feedback collected on every job (post-install survey, NPS, follow-up call)
- [ ] Daily metrics tracked: jobs completed, quality score, customer feedback, crew utilization, any safety incidents

### B) Ramp & Stabilization (Week 2–4)

- [ ] Volume ramp begins based on quality and capacity confirmation
- [ ] Weekly launch metrics review with VP of Expansion (see Section 10.2 for launch metrics)
- [ ] Escalation protocols tested in live conditions — at least one real or simulated escalation run through the full chain
- [ ] Second crew onboarded and in training (if market plan calls for it)
- [ ] Rebate submissions flowing — first rebate applications submitted and confirmed
- [ ] Any launch issues documented in the Lessons Learned log (see Appendix A-8)

### C) Transition Planning (Week 3–4)

- [ ] Stabilization scorecard tracking initiated (see Section 9.2)
- [ ] War room cadence shifts from daily to 3x/week (if metrics support it)
- [ ] Market Launch Lead begins handoff planning — documenting what the GM needs to own independently
- [ ] GM takes increasing ownership of daily operations while Market Launch Lead shifts to oversight

> **Note:** Post-launch duration may extend beyond 4 weeks depending on volume ramp, quality metrics, and stabilization scorecard progress. The Market Launch Lead does not exit until the VP of Expansion confirms the market is on track for stabilization. [TBD — confirm with Brett on formal criteria for Market Launch Lead exit]

## How to Use This Checklist

This checklist is a living document for each market launch. Here's how the Market Launch Lead should operate it:

1. **Copy this checklist** into a dedicated Notion page or project tracker for the specific market launch
2. **Assign owners** to every line item — a task without an owner is a task that won't get done
3. **Add actual dates** next to each item based on the market's specific T-minus countdown
4. **Review weekly** in the launch war room — walk through every item, flag blockers, escalate immediately
5. **Color-code status** — green (done), yellow (in progress / on track), red (at risk / blocked)
6. **Escalate red items** to the VP of Expansion within 24 hours — don't wait for the next weekly review

## If You Only Remember 3 Things

1. **This is the ONE checklist.** Every market launch follows this T-minus countdown. If it's on this list and it's not done, you don't launch. No exceptions, no "we'll handle it post-go-live."
2. **Licensing is the #1 timeline killer.** Start it Day 1 of Phase 2, track it weekly, and escalate early. Everything else can be compressed in a pinch — licensing cannot.
3. **The Go/No-Go decision belongs to the VP of Expansion — and the default answer is No-Go.** A two-week delay costs days. Launching unprepared costs months. When in doubt, don't launch.

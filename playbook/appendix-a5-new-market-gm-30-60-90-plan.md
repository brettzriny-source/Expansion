# Appendix A-5: New Market GM 30/60/90 Plan

> The structured onboarding plan for a GM hired into a market that doesn't exist yet.

## Purpose

This is not the same 30/60/90 as the one in the GM Playbook (Section 2.5). That plan assumes you're walking into a running market — there's a team, a warehouse, a rhythm, and customers. This plan assumes you're walking into a warehouse that might not have racking yet, a crew that hasn't been hired, and a city where Jetson has never done an install.

Building a market from scratch while simultaneously learning how to be a Jetson GM is a different job. The first 90 days in a new market are about learning the Jetson operating system, building a team from zero, proving the operation works before go-live, and then owning it. This plan gives you the sequence, the deliverables, and the support structure to get there.

If you follow this plan, you won't be guessing. You'll know exactly what to focus on every week, who's helping you, and what "done" looks like at each milestone.

> **Core principle:** You are not doing this alone. The Market Launch Lead and the expansion team are your partners for the first 90 days. Your job is to absorb the Jetson system, build the local team, and progressively take ownership — not to figure everything out by yourself. Lean on the support structure. That's what it's for.

---

## 1) First 30 Days — "Learn the Machine + Build the Team"

Your first 30 days are about two things: learning how Jetson operates and starting to build the local team. You are not running the market yet. You are absorbing the system, meeting the people, and getting your hands dirty in an existing market before you start building your own.

### Week 1: Immersion

- Spend 3-5 days at an existing Jetson market shadowing a working GM. Watch morning huddles, ride along on installs, sit in on scheduling reviews, see how Zuper and the warehouse actually work in practice. This is the single most valuable thing you'll do in your first month.
- Read GM Playbook Sections 0-3 cover to cover: org model, role definition, and operating cadence. This is the system you're going to run. Know it cold.
- Meet your Market Launch Lead. Get briefed on the launch status, the T-minus plan, and every open workstream. Understand where the market stands and what's ahead.

### Week 2: Start Building

- Begin local hiring with expansion team support. Your first priority is an FSM and your first crew. The expansion team has the job postings, the comp bands, and the recruiting playbook — use them.
- Tour the facility and warehouse. Understand setup status: is racking installed? Are trucks ordered? Is charging infrastructure in place? Meet the Warehouse Manager if they've been hired.
- Start building relationships with local licensing contacts, inspectors, and utility reps. These relationships matter more than you think — a good relationship with a building inspector saves you weeks over the life of the market.

### Weeks 3-4: First Hires + Systems

- First hires start arriving. Begin coordinating training schedules with Denver (or whatever the current training hub is). Don't skip this — uncertified crews don't touch customer homes.
- Get trained on systems yourself: Zuper (job management), HubSpot (CRM/pipeline), Rippling (HR/timesheets). You need to know these tools as well as your team does.
- Attend the daily war room (if the market has launched) or the pre-launch cross-functional sync. Be present. Learn the cadence. Ask questions.

### 30-Day Deliverable

"30-Day Market Readiness Assessment" — a written report to your Market Launch Lead covering what's on track, what's at risk, and what needs immediate attention. Be honest. This document sets the tone for your relationship with the expansion team.

## 2) Days 31-60 — "Prove the System Works"

The second 30 days are about taking the team you've started building and proving that the operation actually works. By the end of this phase, you should have a certified crew, a stocked warehouse, and enough installs under your belt to know whether the system is running or not.

### Crew & Training

- Crew training is in progress or complete. Your first crew should be certified by the end of this phase. No certified crew, no go-live — this is a hard gate.
- If you're in soft launch, you're participating in test installs alongside your crew. Get in the field. See how the work flows from schedule to truck to jobsite to completion.

### Warehouse & Fleet

- Warehouse is fully stocked with kits and materials. Trucks are ready, wrapped, and charging is operational. If any of this is behind, escalate immediately — this is a launch blocker.

### Operations Cadence

- Start running morning huddles and the daily cadence from GM Playbook Section 3.1. Even if volume is low, build the muscle memory now. The cadence is the foundation of everything.
- Own daily field operations with Market Launch Lead oversight. They're watching, coaching, and catching things you'll miss. That's the point.
- Begin quality audits on every single job. In a new market, 100% audit rate is non-negotiable until you've established the quality bar.
- Build the weekly operating rhythm: Zuper hygiene, timesheet accuracy, schedule reviews, labor efficiency tracking.

### 60-Day Deliverable

"60-Day Ops Report" — quality scores, labor efficiency metrics, staffing status (hired vs. plan), and a clear-eyed assessment of open risks. This is your proof that the operation is coming together.

| Week 5-6 | Crew Certification & Soft Launch | Crew training in progress; first crew certified; participate in test installs / soft launch installs; warehouse fully stocked and trucks ready | First crew certified; warehouse readiness confirmed | Denver training team, Procurement (inventory), Market Launch Lead |

## 3) Days 61-90 — "Own the Market"

This is where you step into full ownership. The Market Launch Lead moves to oversight mode. You're running the show — daily, weekly, monthly cadences, all of it. The goal of this phase is to prove you can run this market independently and that it's ready to transition to GM Playbook governance.

### Full Operations

- Go-live is in progress or complete. You're running full volume operations.
- Running all GM Playbook cadences independently: daily huddles, weekly ops reviews, monthly business reviews. The Market Launch Lead is watching from a distance, not driving.

### Stabilization & Growth

- Stabilization scorecard tracking begins. You and the Market Launch Lead are monitoring the metrics that determine whether this market is "stable" (see Section 9.2).
- Build local reputation from day one of go-live: push for Google reviews on every completed job, respond to every review (especially low-star ones within 24 hours), and start building community presence.
- Second crew hiring and training pipeline is active. A single-crew market is fragile. Start building depth now.

### 90-Day Deliverable

"90-Day Handoff Readiness Assessment" — the big one. Is this market ready for formal transition from Expansion Playbook governance to GM Playbook governance? This assessment covers operational stability, team readiness, quality metrics, financial performance, and open risks. It's the document that triggers the formal handoff process (see Section 9.3 and Appendix A-6).

| **Week** | **Focus Area** | **Key Activities** | **Deliverables** | **Support From** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 9-10 | Full Go-Live & Independent Operations | Full go-live operations; running all GM Playbook cadences independently (daily, weekly, monthly); Market Launch Lead in oversight mode only; stabilization scorecard tracking begins | Stabilization scorecard baseline established | Market Launch Lead (oversight only), Central functions (steady-state support) |
| Week 11-12 | Stabilization & Handoff Preparation | Build local reputation (Google reviews, community presence); second crew hiring and training pipeline active; prepare 90-Day Handoff Readiness Assessment | **90-Day Handoff Readiness Assessment** — is the market ready for formal transition to GM Playbook governance? | Market Launch Lead (assessment review), VP of Expansion (handoff approval) |

## 4) Timing Note: When Does This Clock Start?

The exact timing of this 30/60/90 depends on when you're hired relative to go-live. There are two common scenarios:

### Scenario A: Hired Before Soft Launch (Ideal)

If you're hired 8-12 weeks before soft launch, the 30/60/90 aligns naturally with the launch phases:

- Days 1-30 = Pre-launch build (learning + hiring)
- Days 31-60 = Soft launch (proving the system)
- Days 61-90 = Go-live and stabilization (owning the market)

This is the ideal scenario. You have time to learn the system, build the team, and ramp into ownership gradually.

### Scenario B: Hired At or After Go-Live (Compressed)

If you're hired at or after go-live, the timeline compresses significantly. The Market Launch Lead provides heavier support in the first 30 days — effectively doing the job alongside you while you get up to speed. The immersion at an existing market may be shortened to 1-2 days, and the learning curve is steeper.

In this scenario, expect:

- Days 1-30 = Accelerated learning + immediately owning daily ops with heavy MLL support
- Days 31-60 = Taking full ownership, MLL stepping back
- Days 61-90 = Proving independence, preparing for handoff

Either way, the deliverables are the same. The 30-Day Assessment, 60-Day Ops Report, and 90-Day Handoff Readiness Assessment are required regardless of when you start.

> **Bottom line:** The exact timing depends on when the GM is hired relative to go-live. If hired before soft launch, the 30/60/90 aligns with pre-launch to soft launch to go-live. If hired at or after go-live, the timeline compresses and the Market Launch Lead provides heavier support in the first 30 days. The deliverables don't change — only the pace does.

## If You Only Remember 3 Things

1. **Week 1 immersion at an existing market is the single most valuable thing you'll do.** Shadowing a working GM for 3-5 days teaches you more about how Jetson actually operates than any document ever will. Don't skip it, don't shorten it, don't treat it as optional.
2. **You have a support structure — use it.** The Market Launch Lead, the expansion team, Denver training, central functions — they're all there to help you succeed. The GMs who struggle are the ones who try to figure everything out alone. The ones who thrive lean in hard during the first 60 days and then step into ownership with confidence.
3. **The three deliverables are your proof points.** The 30-Day Market Readiness Assessment, the 60-Day Ops Report, and the 90-Day Handoff Readiness Assessment are not busywork. They force you to honestly assess where you stand, and they give the expansion team the information they need to support you. Write them with candor. Sugar-coating risks helps nobody.

# Appendix A-6: Handoff Checklist — Expansion → Steady-State

> The formal gate between Expansion Playbook governance and GM Playbook governance. No market transitions without completing this checklist.

## Purpose

This is the single checklist that must be completed before a market transitions from Expansion Playbook governance to GM Playbook governance. The handoff is scorecard-gated, not date-driven — no amount of calendar pressure overrides an incomplete checklist. Every item below exists because leaving it undone creates a gap that the incoming GM will have to close while also trying to run a market. That is an unfair tax on a new GM and a setup for early failure. The Market Launch Lead is accountable for completing this checklist. The GM is accountable for reviewing and accepting it. VP of Expansion and VP Field Ops both sign off.

> **Core principle:** The handoff is not a date — it is a scorecard. A market transitions when it earns transition, not when the calendar says so. Every gap you leave behind becomes the GM's problem, and it will cost more to fix later than to finish now.

## 1) Stabilization Scorecard (All Must Be Green)

Before any other checklist item matters, the stabilization scorecard must show green across all dimensions. If any metric is yellow or red, the market is not ready to hand off — full stop.

| Metric | Threshold | Measurement Period |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Labor efficiency** | At or above target [TBD — confirm threshold with Cameron] | 4 consecutive weeks at or above |
| **Callback rate** | Below threshold [TBD — confirm with Cameron] | 4 consecutive weeks below |
| **Customer satisfaction (NPS or CSAT)** | At or above target [TBD — confirm metric and threshold] | Trailing 30 days |
| **Revenue ramp** | On plan or above [TBD — confirm with Finance] | Trailing 4 weeks vs. ramp plan |
| **Staffing** | Minimum viable crew count met and all trained | Current state |
| **Safety / Quality** | No open P1 safety or quality issues | Current state |

> **Non-negotiable:** All six metrics must be green simultaneously. Four green and two yellow is not "close enough." If you can't hit the bar, you're not ready. Extend the stabilization period and fix the gaps.

---

## 2) People & Org

The market must have its core leadership team in place and operating independently before handoff. The expansion team cannot be a crutch.

- **GM is in role and operating independently.** The GM is running the market day-to-day without the Market Launch Lead making decisions for them. If the Market Launch Lead is still the de facto decision-maker, the handoff is premature.
- **FSM (Field Service Manager) is in role.** The GM's right hand for field execution is hired, trained, and functioning.
- **Warehouse Manager is in role.** Inventory, receiving, and facility operations are owned locally.
- **Minimum crew headcount met** [TBD — confirm per-market minimum with Brett/Cameron]. The market has enough certified installers to sustain the current revenue run rate without burning out the team.
- **All field staff certified.** Every installer has completed Denver training and passed certification. No exceptions — uncertified techs do not touch customer homes.
- **GM is running all GM Playbook cadences independently.** Daily standups, weekly team meetings, monthly business reviews — the GM is running them without prompting or facilitation from the expansion team.
- **Reporting lines established.** GM reports to Cameron (VP Field Ops) or the designated regional leader. The expansion team is no longer in the chain of command.

---

## 3) Facility, Fleet, Equipment

Physical infrastructure must be fully operational — not "mostly done" or "waiting on one more thing."

- **Warehouse fully operational.** Racking installed, security system active, network connected, branding and signage complete. The warehouse is not a construction zone.
- **All fleet vehicles received, registered, insured, and operational.** Every vehicle assigned to the market is road-ready. Registration, insurance, and decals are complete.
- **Chargers installed and operational.** EV chargers (if applicable) are installed, tested, and working.
- **Initial BOM/kit inventory stocked and replenishment flow working.** The warehouse has sufficient inventory to support current job volume, and the reorder/replenishment process with Procurement is tested and functioning.
- **Tool inventory complete.** All required tools are on hand, inventoried, and assigned.

---

## 4) Systems & Data

Systems must be configured, tested, and actively in use — not just provisioned.

- **Zuper configured, tested, and in use for all jobs.** Job scheduling, dispatching, time tracking, and job completion workflows are live and being used correctly by the field team.
- **HubSpot pipeline flowing.** Leads are moving through the pipeline (leads → consults → closed won → install ready) without manual workarounds or broken handoffs.
- **Rippling set up for all employees.** Payroll, time tracking, and benefits are configured and verified for every employee in the market.
- **NetSuite inventory configured.** Receiving, consumption, and inventory tracking are working. The warehouse team knows how to use it.
- **Data quality confirmed.** No orphaned records, no jobs stuck in wrong statuses, no duplicate contacts. A clean data foundation for the GM to inherit.

---

## 5) Licensing, Permits, Compliance

Licensing is one of the most common sources of post-handoff fire drills. Get this airtight before you hand off.

- **All required state and municipal licenses obtained.** Contractor licenses, business licenses, and any trade-specific permits are in hand — not "in process."
- **License renewal dates documented with responsible owner.** Every license has a renewal date, and someone specific owns the renewal. This does not default to "the GM will figure it out."
- **Insurance and bonding confirmed and documented.** General liability, workers' comp, auto, and any required bonding are active and documented.
- **Key licensing contacts documented.** The GM knows who to call at the state licensing board, municipality, and insurance broker.

---

## 6) Rebates & Incentives

Rebate programs are market-specific and often complex. The GM and Customer Ops team need to inherit a clean, working process.

- **All relevant rebate programs enrolled.** Federal, state, utility, and municipal incentive programs applicable to this market are identified and enrollment is complete.
- **Rebate process documented and tested end-to-end.** At least one rebate has been submitted, tracked, and received (or the process has been validated with the program administrator).
- **Rebate program contacts documented.** Names, phone numbers, and email addresses for every rebate program the market participates in.
- **Sales and Customer Ops trained on market-specific rebate rules.** The people quoting and processing jobs in this market understand what's available, eligibility rules, and how to submit.

---

## 7) Documentation & Knowledge Transfer

If it's not written down, it didn't happen. The GM should not have to reverse-engineer anything the expansion team learned during launch.

- **Service area map finalized and shared with Sales + Customer Ops.** The geographic boundaries of the market are defined, documented, and the teams selling and scheduling work know them.
- **Municipality-specific permitting notes documented.** Every municipality in the service area that has unique permitting requirements, inspection processes, or quirks is documented.
- **Lessons learned from launch captured** (see Appendix A-8). What went well, what went sideways, and what the next launch should do differently.
- **Open issues and risk register handed to GM.** Anything unresolved — vendor issues, pending permits, known quality concerns — is documented and formally transferred.
- **All vendor/supplier contacts documented.** Every vendor relationship the expansion team established for this market is documented with contact info and account details.
- **Emergency contacts and after-hours procedures documented.** The GM knows who to call, when, and for what — including after-hours escalation paths.

---

## 8) Sign-Off

The handoff is not complete until four people sign off. This is not bureaucracy — it is accountability.

- **Market Launch Lead signs off** that all checklist items are complete (or explicitly documented as exceptions with mitigation plans).
- **GM signs off** that they have received and reviewed all documentation, systems access, and open items.
- **VP of Expansion (Brett Zriny) approves** the formal transition. This confirms that the expansion team's job is done for this market.
- **VP Field Ops (Cameron) accepts** the market into the steady-state portfolio. This confirms that Field Ops is ready to support the market under GM Playbook governance.

## If You Only Remember 3 Things

1. **The handoff is scorecard-gated, not date-driven.** All six stabilization metrics must be green simultaneously. Calendar pressure does not override an incomplete scorecard.
2. **Every checklist item exists because skipping it creates a problem downstream.** Licensing gaps, missing documentation, untrained staff, broken systems — these all become the GM's emergency if you leave them unfinished. Do not hand off a market with known gaps.
3. **Four signatures close the handoff: Market Launch Lead, GM, VP of Expansion, VP Field Ops.** This is not a formality. Each signer is confirming that their piece of the transition is complete and they are ready for what comes next.

> **Do NOT hand off a market with incomplete items.** Every gap you leave becomes the GM's problem — and it will cost more to fix later than to finish now. If you're tempted to hand off early because the timeline says so, re-read the first principle: the handoff is a scorecard, not a date.

# Appendix A-7: Launch War Room Agenda Template

> The standing agenda and operating rules for Jetson's daily launch sync — the highest-tempo meeting in the expansion cycle.

## Purpose

The war room is a forcing function, not a status update. During the launch phase, things break fast and decisions stack up. The war room exists to surface blockers, make calls, and assign owners — every single day. It is the shortest path between "something is wrong" and "someone is fixing it by a specific date."

This template defines the standing agenda, the meeting rules, and the action tracker format. If you are the Market Launch Lead, this is your meeting to own. If you are an attendee, show up on time, report your one blocker or say "clear," and leave with your assignments. That is it.

This appendix supports Section 8.4 (Launch War Room Protocol) with the tactical, copy-and-use template.

> **Core Principle: 30 Minutes, Hard Cap. Every Time.**
> If the war room goes over 30 minutes, you are solving problems in the meeting instead of assigning them. The war room identifies what is broken and who will fix it. The actual fixing happens afterward, with an owner and a due date. If a topic takes more than 2 minutes to discuss, it goes offline. No exceptions.

---

## 1. Meeting Card

| **Field** | **Detail** |
| --- | --- |
| **Meeting Name** | Launch War Room |
| **Owner** | Market Launch Lead |
| **Frequency** | Daily during soft launch and first 2 weeks of go-live; 3x/week for weeks 3-4; 2x/week during stabilization; then retired at handoff |
| **Duration** | 30 minutes (hard cap) |
| **Attendees** | Market Launch Lead, GM, FSM, Sales rep (market), Customer Ops rep (PC), Procurement/WM rep, Support rep |
| **Format** | Standing meeting (literally — keep it fast) |

The cadence steps down as the market stabilizes. Daily is non-negotiable during soft launch and the first two weeks of go-live — that is when the most things break. As the market finds its rhythm, the war room downshifts to 3x, then 2x per week, and then it is retired entirely at handoff to steady-state. If you are still running a daily war room in week 6, something is wrong with the launch, not the cadence.

---

## 2. Agenda — Block 1: Metrics Flash (5 min)

This is the fastest block. The Market Launch Lead or GM reads the numbers. No commentary, no root-cause analysis — just the facts.

- **Yesterday's jobs completed vs. planned.** Did we hit the number or not?
- **Quality issues or customer complaints from yesterday.** Any callbacks, escalations, or NPS detractors?
- **Staffing status for today.** Are we fully crewed or short? Any call-outs?
- **Pipeline and scheduling status for next 5 days.** Are we booked? Are there gaps? Is demand outpacing capacity?

If a number is off, it becomes a blocker in Block 2. Do not start diagnosing it here.

---

## 3. Agenda — Block 2: Blockers and Escalations (10 min)

This is the core of the war room. Each function reports ONE blocker or risk — or says "clear." Round-robin, same order every day:

1. **Sales** — Pipeline, lead flow, scheduling issues
2. **Customer Ops** — Scheduling, customer communication, handoff gaps
3. **Field Ops** — Crew, quality, job completion, safety
4. **Warehouse/Procurement** — Material availability, fleet, equipment
5. **Support** — Customer escalations, post-install issues

The rules are simple: state the blocker, state what you need, and move on. No solving in the meeting. If it takes more than 2 minutes, the Market Launch Lead assigns an owner, sets a due date, and it goes on the tracker. The discussion happens offline, after the war room.

---

## 4. Agenda — Block 3: Decisions Needed (10 min)

Some blockers require a cross-functional decision — not just an owner, but an actual call that affects multiple teams. This block is for those items.

- Any attendee can raise a decision item.
- The Market Launch Lead makes the call, or escalates to the VP of Expansion if it exceeds their authority.
- Decisions are recorded in the tracker with the decision, the rationale (one sentence), and the owner for execution.

If no decisions are needed, this block is skipped and the time rolls into Block 4. Do not fill time for the sake of filling time.

---

## 5. Agenda — Block 4: Actions and Owners (5 min)

The Market Launch Lead recaps every action item from today's war room:

- **New items:** What was assigned, to whom, by when.
- **Carryover items:** Anything from yesterday that is still open. If it is overdue, the owner explains why and gives a new date — in one sentence.

Every action has ONE owner and a due date. No "we should look into this." No "the team will figure it out." One name. One date. That is the standard.

---

## 6. War Room Rules

These rules are non-negotiable. Print them. Tape them to the wall.

1. **Start on time.** Do not wait for stragglers. If someone is late, they catch up from the tracker afterward.
2. **No laptops** except the person running the tracker. Everyone else is present and engaged.
3. **The 2-minute rule.** If a topic takes more than 2 minutes to discuss, it goes offline with an owner assigned. The war room is for surfacing, not solving.
4. **Every action has ONE owner and a due date.** No "we should..." — only "who will... by when."
5. **The Market Launch Lead owns the tracker** and follows up on overdue items. If something is overdue two days in a row, it escalates automatically.

The Market Launch Lead maintains this tracker and shares it after every war room. This is the single source of truth for who owes what.

**Status definitions:** Open = not started. In Progress = underway. Complete = done and verified. Escalated = moved up to VP of Expansion.

---

## 3 Takeaways

**1. The war room is a forcing function, not a status update.** Its job is to surface blockers and assign owners in 30 minutes flat. If you are solving problems in the room, you are doing it wrong.

**2. Every action has ONE owner and a due date.** "We should look into that" is not an action item. "Sarah will resolve the permit issue by Thursday" is. One name. One date. No ambiguity.

**3. The cadence steps down, then the war room dies.** Daily during launch, 3x during ramp, 2x during stabilization, then retired at handoff. If you are still running daily war rooms six weeks in, the problem is the launch, not the meeting schedule.

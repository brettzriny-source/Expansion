# Appendix A-2: Expansion Weekly Rhythm Template

> The Market Launch Lead's weekly operating cadence — from pre-launch through stabilization and handoff.

## Purpose

Launching a market is not one heroic sprint. It is a series of weekly cycles, each one tightening the readiness of the next. This template defines the Market Launch Lead's weekly rhythm across three distinct phases: pre-launch build, active launch, and stabilization. Each phase has a different intensity, different meeting cadence, and different set of outputs. The rhythm changes because the work changes — and knowing when to shift gears is what separates a controlled launch from a chaotic one.

> **Core rule:** Your weekly rhythm is not a suggestion — it is a commitment device. If you skip the Tuesday cross-functional sync during pre-launch, you will discover the gap on launch day. If you skip the Friday metrics report during launch, the VP of Expansion will discover the gap for you.

---

## 1) Pre-Launch Phase (T-90 to T-0)

This is the build phase. The market does not exist yet. Your job is to stand up every workstream on time and surface risks early. The weekly cadence is structured around project management discipline — checking every T-minus item, coordinating cross-functional dependencies, and reporting status upward.

### A) Weekly Schedule

| Day | Activity | Time Est. | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Monday** | Workstream status review — walk every T-minus item due this week and next week. Update project tracker with current status, owners, and blockers. | 2 hrs | Updated project tracker, blocker list |
| **Tuesday** | Cross-functional sync (30 min) with reps from Sales, Customer Ops, and Procurement. Review dependencies, confirm handoff timing, flag items that need escalation. | 30 min + 30 min prep | Meeting notes, action items with owners and due dates |
| **Wednesday** | Hiring pipeline review — check status of GM search, field team recruiting, warehouse manager candidates. Facility and fleet progress check — lease status, build-out timeline, truck orders, charger installation. | 1.5 hrs | Hiring tracker update, facility/fleet status update |
| **Thursday** | Licensing, permits, and rebate enrollment follow-up — chase any outstanding municipal or state approvals. Systems provisioning check — confirm Zuper, HubSpot, Rippling, and NetSuite setup progress with IT. | 1.5 hrs | Licensing tracker update, systems readiness checklist |
| **Friday** | Weekly launch status report to VP of Expansion. Update the risk register — add new risks, update probability/impact scores, close resolved items. Plan next week's priorities. | 2 hrs | Weekly status report (sent to Brett), updated risk register |

### B) Pre-Launch Phase Rules

- Total structured time: approximately 8 hours per week. The rest is execution — chasing items, visiting the facility, interviewing candidates, solving problems.
- The Monday workstream review is solo. Do it before you talk to anyone else. You need to know the truth before you share it.
- The Tuesday cross-functional sync must be short and tight. Send the agenda by Monday EOD. If there is nothing to discuss, cancel it — but that should be rare during active build.
- The Friday report to the VP of Expansion follows a fixed format: green/yellow/red by workstream, top 3 risks, top 3 wins, help needed. Keep it to one page.

## 2) Launch Phase (Weeks 1–8 Post Go-Live)

The market is live. Real customers. Real jobs. Real problems. The cadence tightens significantly. The war room runs daily. Other activities layer on top of the daily rhythm.

### A) Daily Cadence: War Room

During the launch phase, the war room meets every day. Monday through Friday, same time, same format. This is the heartbeat of the launch.

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Frequency** | Daily, Monday through Friday |
| **Duration** | 30 min (hard stop) |
| **Attendees** | Market Launch Lead, GM, Sales rep, Customer Ops rep, Procurement rep, Support rep (as needed) |
| **Format** | Metrics flash (5 min) → blockers (10 min) → action items (10 min) → escalations (5 min) |
| **Time Est.** | 30 min/day = 2.5 hrs/week |

> **Non-negotiable:** The war room is daily during launch. Not three times a week. Not "as needed." Daily. The MA launch proved that problems compound overnight when you skip a day. See Section 8.4 for the full war room protocol.

### B) Weekly Activities (Layered on Top of Daily War Room)

| Day | Weekly Activity (in addition to daily war room) | Time Est. | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Monday** | Week-setting: review weekend metrics (if weekend jobs ran), set the week's focus areas and targets. Monday's war room is longer — 45 min instead of 30. | 45 min (extended war room) + 1 hr metrics review | Weekly focus areas, weekend metrics summary |
| **Tuesday** | Quality review — audit every completed job from the prior week. Check photos, permits filed, customer communication, callback triggers. Flag patterns. | 2–3 hrs (depends on volume) | Quality audit log, pattern report, coaching notes for GM |
| **Wednesday** | Hiring and staffing check — are we on pace for crew ramp? Any attrition? GM 1:1 — dedicated time to coach, align, and hear what the GM is seeing on the ground. | 1 hr (staffing) + 1 hr (GM 1:1) | Staffing tracker update, GM 1:1 notes |
| **Thursday** | Customer feedback review — check NPS/CSAT scores, read every customer comment, identify recovery opportunities. Sales ramp check — is pipeline building? Are we converting? Is volume matching capacity? | 1.5 hrs | Customer feedback summary, sales ramp report |
| **Friday** | Weekly launch metrics report to VP of Expansion. Lessons learned capture — what did we learn this week that changes how we operate? Plan next week. | 2 hrs | Weekly metrics report (sent to Brett), lessons learned log, next-week plan |

### C) Launch Phase Rules

- Total structured time: approximately 15–18 hours per week (daily war room + weekly activities). This is the most intense period. Plan for it.
- The Tuesday quality review is the most important weekly activity after the war room. During ramp, every job matters. One bad install in a new market can define your reputation for months.
- The Wednesday GM 1:1 is protected time. Do not cancel it. The GM is building a market from scratch and needs a thought partner, not just a task tracker.
- The Friday lessons learned capture is not optional. If you wait until post-launch to capture lessons, you will forget 80% of them. Write them down while they are fresh.
- Core rule: During launch phase, the Market Launch Lead should be physically present in the market at least 3 days per week. You cannot run a launch from a screen.

## 3) Stabilization Phase (Week 8+ Until Handoff)

The market is running. Volume is ramping. The team is finding its footing. Your job shifts from building and firefighting to coaching, monitoring, and preparing for handoff. The cadence relaxes to match the GM Playbook's weekly rhythm — because you are training the GM to run independently.

### A) Weekly Schedule

| Day | Activity | Time Est. | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Monday** | Stabilization scorecard review — check all stabilization metrics (callback rate, revenue ramp, retention, NPS, labor efficiency). Identify any metric trending yellow or red. | 1 hr | Scorecard update, flag list |
| **Wednesday** | GM 1:1 — this is now the primary touchpoint. Review the GM's own weekly rhythm (per GM Playbook Appendix A-2), coach on gaps, discuss escalations. The GM should be running the meeting; you are the sounding board. | 1 hr | GM 1:1 notes, coaching log |
| **Friday** | Weekly stabilization report to VP of Expansion — lighter than launch phase. Focus on scorecard progress, handoff readiness, and timeline to transition. Begin formal handoff preparation (Appendix A-6 checklist). | 1 hr | Stabilization report (sent to Brett), handoff checklist progress |

### B) Stabilization Phase Rules

- Total structured time: approximately 3–4 hours per week. The rest of your bandwidth frees up for the next market launch.
- The daily war room stops. If you are still running a daily war room past week 8, something is wrong — either the market is not stabilizing or you are not letting go.
- The GM should now be running their own weekly rhythm per the GM Playbook. Your Wednesday 1:1 is a check on whether that rhythm is working, not a replacement for it.
- Core rule: If the stabilization scorecard has any red metrics past week 12, escalate to the VP of Expansion. The market may need intervention before handoff can proceed.
- Physical presence drops to 1 day per week or less. The GM owns the floor now.
- Handoff preparation is real work. The checklist in Appendix A-6 is detailed. Start it at week 8, not the week before you plan to hand off.

## 4) Phase Comparison: At a Glance

| Dimension | Pre-Launch (T-90 to T-0) | Launch (Weeks 1–8) | Stabilization (Week 8+) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Weekly structured hours** | ~8 hrs | ~15–18 hrs | ~3–4 hrs |
| **Meeting cadence** | Weekly | Daily war room + weekly activities | 2–3 touchpoints per week |
| **Primary role** | Project manager | Launch operator | Coach and auditor |
| **Physical presence** | As needed (facility visits, interviews) | 3+ days/week in market | 1 day/week or less |
| **Key output** | Weekly status report + risk register | Weekly metrics report + lessons learned | Stabilization report + handoff checklist |
| **Reports to VP of Expansion** | Friday status report | Friday metrics report | Friday stabilization report |

## 5) Transition Triggers: When to Shift Phases

Do not shift your cadence based on the calendar. Shift it based on what is actually happening.

### A) Pre-Launch → Launch

You shift to launch cadence when the market goes live — first real customer job is scheduled or completed. This is not when you "feel ready." It is when the first job hits.

### B) Launch → Stabilization

You shift to stabilization cadence when all of the following are true:

- The GM is hired, onboarded, and running daily operations independently
- The daily war room has been stable (no fire drills) for at least 2 consecutive weeks
- Weekly job volume is at or above the soft launch target
- No red metrics on the launch scorecard for 2 consecutive weeks
- The GM confirms they are ready to run independently with weekly oversight only

If any of these are not true at week 8, you stay in launch cadence. Do not relax prematurely.

### C) Stabilization → Handoff

You exit when the stabilization scorecard criteria (Section 9.2) are met and the handoff checklist (Appendix A-6) is complete. The market transitions to GM Playbook governance. You are done.

---

## If You Only Remember 3 Things

1. **Three phases, three rhythms.** Pre-launch is weekly project management (~8 hrs). Launch is daily war room plus weekly deep dives (~15–18 hrs). Stabilization is weekly coaching and scorecard monitoring (~3–4 hrs). Match the intensity to the phase.
2. **The war room is daily during launch — no exceptions.** Problems compound overnight in a new market. The daily 30-minute war room is the cheapest insurance you have against a launch going sideways.
3. **Shift phases based on conditions, not calendar dates.** Do not relax to stabilization cadence because it has been 8 weeks. Relax because the GM is running independently, volume is on target, and the scorecard is clean. Conditions, not dates.
